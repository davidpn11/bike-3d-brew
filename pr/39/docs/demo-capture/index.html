Demo: capture a real bike and view it in 3D

From the iPhone app to the web viewer, step by step. It takes about an hour the first time: 30 minutes of setup, 15 minutes of capture, a few minutes for the phone to build the model, then the viewer.

How capture works. The app does not record a video. Apple's Object Capture takes photos automatically while you walk slowly around the bike, and the phone builds the 3D model itself. Nothing is processed on a server. Turning an ordinary video from any phone into 3D is not built yet; see the last section.

First time on a real device. The app has only been compiled by CI so far. If a step fails, copy the exact error into Claude Code or Codex in this repo ("the iOS app fails with: …") and the ios-engineer fixes it.

What you need

  • An iPhone Pro with LiDAR (12 Pro or any later Pro / Pro Max) on iOS 18 or newer. Object Capture needs LiDAR, so a non-Pro iPhone will not work.
  • A Mac with Xcode 16 or newer. Xcode is free on the Mac App Store. It is about 10 GB, so start the download first.
  • A USB-C cable for the first install, and your Apple ID. A free account works; a paid developer account is not needed.
  • A bike on a kickstand or work stand, a plain matte floor, and soft, even light. An overcast day outdoors is ideal.

1. Install the app on your iPhone

  1. Install Xcode from the Mac App Store and open it once. Accept the license, and install the iOS platform when it asks.
  2. Point the command-line tools at Xcode:
  3. Add your Apple ID in Xcode → Settings → Accounts → + → Apple ID.
  4. Generate the project. xcodegen is already installed on this Mac: brew install xcodegen otherwise.
  5. Signing:
    • Click the BikeCapture target → Signing & Capabilities.
    • Tick Automatically manage signing and pick your Personal Team under Team.
    • If Xcode says the bundle identifier is taken, change com.bike3dbrew.capture to something unique, for example com.<yourname>.bikecapture.
  6. Prepare the iPhone:
    1. Connect it by cable, unlock it and tap Trust.
    2. Turn on Settings → Privacy & Security → Developer Mode. The phone restarts; confirm when it is back.
  7. Run: pick your iPhone in Xcode's device menu at the top, then press ⌘R.
  8. First launch: iOS blocks apps from a free account until you trust it, in Settings → General → VPN & Device Management → your Apple ID → Trust. Then open Bike Capture and allow camera access.

With a free Apple ID the app stops opening after 7 days. Plug the phone in and press ⌘R again to renew it.

2. Capture the bike

Before you start:

  • Put the bike on its kickstand or a work stand, in the middle of a space you can walk all the way around.
  • Use a matte floor, with no mirrors or glass behind the bike.
  • Use soft, even light: overcast outdoors, or a bright room with no hard shadows.
  • Shiny paint and chrome come out poorly. A polarizing filter and diffuse light help; use a dulling spray only if the bike's owner agrees.
  • The bike must not move. Object Capture tracks the phone against the room, so a turntable does not work.

In the app:

  1. Tap + and give the bike a name.
  2. Choose Object mode, which removes the background automatically. Use Area mode only if the app cannot find the bike; you then crop it later in the viewer.
  3. Fit the box around the whole bike, including the wheels and the bars, then tap Start capture.
  4. Walk slowly all the way around, holding the phone steady. Tap Checklist to see the plan:
    • Three orbits: one at hub height, one at saddle height, one from above. Each ticks itself when the pass completes and shows its shot count.
    • Close-ups: fork crown, dropouts, rear derailleur and cockpit. Tick these yourself.
  5. Follow the on-screen hints ("move slower", "move closer"). A good capture has about 100–200 shots in 10–15 minutes.
  6. Tap Finish, then Build 3D model. Keep the app open and in the foreground; it takes a few minutes.
  7. Check the result with View in 3D / AR.

3. Get the capture onto your Mac

  1. In the app, open the bike → Export capture bundle.
  2. AirDrop it to your Mac. It lands in Downloads as <name>.bikecapture.zip. The zip holds model.usdz, poses.json, the photos and a manifest.

Never commit the capture to this repo, not even your own bike (AGENTS.md: generated fixtures only). Keep it in Downloads or another folder outside the repo.

4. Let Claude or Codex check it (optional)

The model is already built on the phone, so there is nothing to process. An agent can sanity-check the bundle and open it for you. In Claude Code (or Codex) in this repo, say:

Check ~/Downloads/<name>.bikecapture.zip: list its contents and manifest, start the viewer locally, open the bundle and tell me if anything looks wrong (orientation, size, missing textures, close-up photos). Do not copy the capture into the repo.

That covers the manual-step checks in MANUAL-STEPS.md: USDZ loads, upright, size in meters, textures. Report anything odd and the agent turns it into a fix with a synthetic test fixture, never with your capture.

5. Open it in the web viewer

  1. Open the viewer at https://davidpn11.github.io/bike-3d-brew/app/, or locally with bun run dev → http://localhost:5190.

  2. Drag the .bikecapture.zip onto the page, or use Open…. On the iPhone, open the viewer in Safari → Open… → Files → the zip.

    The capture stays in your browser only (it is cached, so a reload reopens it). Nothing is uploaded.

  3. Check the basics: the bike stands upright, front to the right. If not, fix it in Edit → Model (Flip / Turn 180°). For an Area-mode capture, use Edit → Model → Crop → Fit to bike.

  4. Make it a demo:

    1. In Edit, double-click a part to orbit around it, zoom toward the pointer, then + Save current view as area (or Set view on an existing area).
    2. Tap the bike to pin comments.
    3. Fill in the Spec sheet.

  5. Switch to Present and step through the areas. Open an area to see its close-up photos from the capture.

Sharing a link with someone else needs the scene published on a host. node tools/publish-scene.mjs <zip> --out <folder> makes a folder you can upload. Ask before putting a real bike on the public GitHub Pages site.

6. Record the demo

  • The capture, on the iPhone: Control Center → Screen Recording. Record steps 2.3–2.7: the box, the orbits, the checklist and the 3D preview.
  • The viewer, on the Mac: press ⌘⇧5 → Record Selected Portion around the browser window.
  • A 2-minute script:
    1. The capture: a 20-second time-lapse of walking around the bike.
    2. Drop the zip on the viewer: the bike appears without a background.
    3. Orbit, then double-click the rear derailleur.
    4. Present mode through 3–4 areas, with their comments and close-up photos.
    5. The customer view on a phone.
  • A turntable video of the bike comes straight from the viewer: Snapshot → 360° video.

If you only have a video

Turning an ordinary video (any phone, no LiDAR) into a model is planned but not built yet (ROADMAP, Milestone 1.5: ingest and reconstruct --backend object-capture). On an Apple silicon Mac like this one, the shortest path is:

  1. Extract sharp frames with ffmpeg.
  2. Run Apple's PhotogrammetrySession on the Mac.
  3. Package the result with tools/reconstruct/cli.mjs package into the same .bikecapture.zip.

Ask the architect to build it ("build the video → 3D path on the Mac"). Until then, the iPhone app is the way to get a real bike into the viewer.
