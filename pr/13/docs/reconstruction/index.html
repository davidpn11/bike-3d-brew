Photos & video → 3D: reconstruction options for bikes

"We can have the best 3D experience, but if it doesn't come from a collection of photos and videos of a bike, it's not useful." (owner)

Goal: any photo set or video of a bike (any camera) → a bike-capture/1 bundle the viewer opens: a model (GLB preferred), poses.json, images/ and manifest.json. Today the only producer is the iOS Object Capture app.

Status of the facts below (2026-10-27). This first pass comes from the author's own knowledge of these tools. It was not re-checked against vendor pages in this session because the budget was cut short. Prices, limits and terms of cloud services change often: check each verify: item before anyone commits money or customer photos. What was checked in the sandbox: Ubuntu 24.04 ships colmap 3.9.1 in universe (apt-get install colmap works, CPU build without CUDA). There is no Docker daemon and no GPU here, so OpenMVS and dense reconstruction were not run.

Notes:

  • Object Capture is the only option with automatic object masking built in, which matters for the "background-free" goal. With COLMAP the bundle needs masking (rembg / U²-Net on the photos before MVS, or cropping the mesh to the dominant cluster after).
  • PhotogrammetrySession takes a folder URL of images from any camera. EXIF focal length helps. Depth and gravity metadata (iPhone LiDAR HEIC) improve scale and orientation. Without them the scale is arbitrary and the model may come out tilted, so auto-orient (ROADMAP M2) becomes required.
  • COLMAP poses are world→camera with OpenCV axes (+X right, +Y down, +Z forward). The viewer wants camera→model with camera looking down -Z, +Y up. So: T_c2w = inverse([R|t]) = [Rᵀ | -Rᵀt], then flip the camera's Y and Z axes: pose = T_c2w · diag(1, -1, -1, 1). COLMAP quaternions are QW QX QY QZ (Hamilton, world→camera).
  • Splats need a viewer path (Spark / @sparkjsdev/spark) and a crop box; pins and areas still work because they only need a surface to raycast against (a proxy mesh or splat-depth picking).

Recommendation

  1. Default: Object Capture on a Mac (tools/reconstruct/macos/ Swift CLI over an image folder, then package to bike-capture/1 with poses.json from the .poses request). It is free, gives the best masked, textured mesh for this object class, and works with photos from any camera and with frames pulled from a video. Detail .full for the web (then GLB + decimate), .raw only for archiving.
  2. Fallback: COLMAP + OpenMVS in Docker (Linux / CI / a rented GPU box) for when no Mac is available: same ingest → reconstruct → package flow, plus mesh cropping to the bike. Expect worse wheels and paint.
  3. Photoreal later: Gaussian splats from the same video (COLMAP poses + gsplat/nerfstudio, or Polycam/Luma/Scaniverse export) once the viewer loads .spz/.ply splats.
  4. Cloud API only after the owner picks a vendor, a budget and accepts its data terms.
  5. AI image-to-3D: never as "the bike". Only for placeholders, clearly labeled.

Capture protocol (photos)

  • Setup: bike on a stand or a turntable, wheels off the ground or the kickstand hidden; plain matte backdrop that differs from the paint; textured floor or newspaper so the poses solve; soft, even light (two softboxes at 45°, or overcast outdoors), no direct sun, no flash.
  • Camera: any camera ≥ 12 MP; fixed zoom (no zooming between shots); f/8–f/11 for depth of field; ISO as low as possible; shutter ≥ 1/125 s handheld (tripod: anything); lock focus and exposure per ring; RAW+JPEG or high-quality JPEG; no portrait mode or beauty filters.
  • Shots: 120–200 total, 70–80 % overlap between neighbors:
    • Ring 1, hub height (~0.35 m), distance ~1.5–2 m: 36–48 shots (every 7.5–10°).
    • Ring 2, saddle height (~0.9 m), looking slightly down: 36–48 shots.
    • Ring 3, high (~1.6 m), looking down at 45°: 24–36 shots.
    • Close-ups (0.4–0.6 m), 4–6 shots each, overlapping each other: drivetrain (both sides of the rear derailleur, chainring), fork crown and dropouts, cockpit (bar, stem, shifters), saddle and seat clamp, each wheel hub and a sector of spokes from both sides.
  • Turntable vs walk-around: turntable with a fixed camera is fastest and steadiest, but the background must stay out of frame or be masked (static background + rotating bike confuses SfM). Walk-around works anywhere and is better for the floor features. Object Capture handles both.
  • Reflective paint: polarizer on the lens (and ideally on the lights), more diffuse light, or a removable dulling spray if the shop agrees. Record which one you used in the manifest notes.

Capture protocol (video)

  • 4K (3840×2160) at 30 fps, or 60 fps if the light allows a fast shutter; HEVC or H.264, not HDR / Dolby Vision (tone mapping varies between decoders); lock exposure and focus; stabilization on; no zooming.
  • Shutter speed ≥ 1/250 s (set it in a pro camera app such as Blackmagic Camera; the stock iOS app picks it automatically), otherwise motion blur kills features on spokes and cables.
  • Slow orbit: about 60–90 s per ring (≈ 4–6°/s), three rings as above, then slow passes over the close-up areas. 3–5 minutes of footage in total.
  • The ingest step extracts frames, keeps the sharpest frame per short time window (variance of the Laplacian) and only adds a frame when the view has changed enough, aiming for 80–200 frames.
